You spend your saved resources, your hero's Power goes up, and you return to the same battle expecting a different result. Then your damage dealer barely scratches the tank. Or your own tank disappears before your healer can act. That is frustrating, especially when the upgrade looked right on the screen.

I'm Alexandre, and my advice is to start with what happened in the fight. A useful upgrade solves a specific problem. A hero who dies before casting needs something different from a hero who survives comfortably but cannot finish an enemy.

Here, I will walk you through the three main attributes and all 14 secondary stats in Hero Wars Alliance. We will use small, clear examples before looking at the formulas. You do not need to memorize the math. I want you to understand what you are buying with your next upgrade.

Why more Power does not guarantee a win

I use Power as a quick overview of development. It cannot tell me whether my damage dealer can get through this opponent's Armor, whether my tank has the right defense, or whether a control effect will interrupt my important skill.

Imagine two upgrades for a hero fighting a physical damage team: one adds Magic Defense, the other adds Armor. Both develop the hero, but Armor addresses the damage in this example. Against a magical team, the decision changes. The opponent gives a stat its context.

My first question: “What stops this hero from doing their job?” Watch who dies first, which attack hurts them, and whether your main skill actually goes off. Those observations are more useful than choosing whichever upgrade adds the largest Power number.

Main stats: the bonuses behind the bonuses

A hero has Strength, Intelligence, and Agility, but only one is their main stat. All three can provide benefits. The main-stat designation adds one extra point of Physical Attack for each point of that particular attribute.

Strength: a larger Health pool

An extra 100 Strength gives 4,000 Health. If Strength is that hero's main stat, it also gives 100 Physical Attack. Strength itself does not add Armor. More Health lets the hero take more damage; Armor reduces the physical damage taken. We will compare those jobs shortly.

Intelligence: magic strength and magic protection

An extra 100 Intelligence gives 300 Magic Attack and 100 Magic Defense. An Intelligence-main hero also receives 100 Physical Attack. I like this combination when both a hero's skill scaling and their need for magical protection matter, but I still compare the actual bonus and resource cost with other upgrades.

Agility: check whether it is the main stat

An extra 100 Agility gives any hero 200 Physical Attack and 100 Armor. For an Agility-main hero, the Physical Attack gain becomes 300. That extra 100 is the main-stat bonus, not a separate fourth attribute.

This explains why I look closely at Agility upgrades for heroes such as Eva: one upgrade helps physical offense and physical defense. It does not automatically make Agility the best purchase at every stage. A specific penetration or survival problem can still be more urgent.

Health: give your hero time to contribute

Health is the amount of damage your hero can withstand before dying. When it reaches zero, the hero dies. It is easy to understand, but the decision to invest in it deserves a little more thought.

Suppose a hero has 100,000 Health and receives repeated hits of 25,000 damage after defenses. With no healing or other effects, four hits defeat them. At 120,000 Health, they survive the fourth hit with 20,000 remaining. That extra action window can be the difference between casting a skill and contributing nothing.

Now suppose the hero already survives until the fight ends, but your team runs out of time. More Health may not solve that loss. This is why my advice is to build enough survival for the job, then examine what improves the hero's contribution.

Read whose Health the skill uses. A shield based on your hero's Health can grow with their Health upgrades. Damage based on the enemy's maximum Health uses the enemy's value. Raising your own Health does not increase that percentage-based damage.

Attack: read the skill before choosing the upgrade

Physical Attack

Physical Attack contributes to basic-attack damage and skills that scale with it. Physical damage must then pass through the target's Armor. The number on your hero screen is therefore not a promise of how much Health every hit will remove.

Take a hypothetical skill that deals 50% of Physical Attack + 2,000. At 20,000 Physical Attack, its damage before defenses is 12,000. Add 4,000 Physical Attack and the skill gains 2,000 damage, reaching 14,000. The upgrade is multiplied by the skill's 50% coefficient; it is not added in full.

Magic Attack

Magic Attack strengthens skills that use it, including magical damage and other effects with Magic Attack scaling. A healing or shielding skill may benefit even though it is not an attack. Read its description and formula to see exactly what improves.

The stat that scales a skill and the type of damage it deals are separate questions. Cornelius is a useful example: his ultimate deals physical damage but scales with Magic Attack. Calling a hero “physical” or “magical” can hide details like this.

How I read a skill: first I check its damage type, then the stat in its formula, and then the target or effect. That tells me which attack stat raises the number and which enemy defense will reduce it.

Defense and penetration: why a strong hit can feel weak

Armor protects against physical damage

Armor reduces physical damage from attacks and abilities. With 3,000 Armor still effective after penetration, a 10,000-damage physical hit becomes 5,000. With 1,000 effective Armor, that same hit becomes 7,500.

Magic Defense protects against magical damage

Magic Defense follows the same calculation for magical damage. It does not replace Armor against physical attacks. Pure damage is a separate damage type; Armor and Magic Defense are not the defenses to use for this calculation.

Effective defense means the defense left after the attacker's matching penetration is applied. These examples isolate that calculation: no critical hits, shields, Toughness, or other modifiers.

Show me the defense formula

Damage reduction = 1 − 1 ÷ (1 + effective defense ÷ 3,000).

The result is a fraction. Multiply by 100 to express it as a percentage. An equivalent way to calculate the hit is: damage received = damage before defense ÷ (1 + effective defense ÷ 3,000).

Armor Penetration removes Armor from the calculation

Each point of Armor Penetration ignores one point of the target's Armor. Against 8,000 Armor, 6,000 penetration leaves 2,000 effective Armor. A 10,000-damage physical hit then deals 6,000 damage, before other effects.

Without penetration, that hit against 8,000 Armor would deal only about 2,727 damage. That is why an attacker can feel much stronger after a penetration upgrade even when their Physical Attack does not change.

Magic Penetration does the same job against Magic Defense

Subtract Magic Penetration from the target's Magic Defense, then calculate magical damage using the defense left over. Physical and magical penetration are not interchangeable.

Effective defense stops at zero. With 10,000 penetration against 5,000 matching defense, the result is zero, not minus 5,000. Excess penetration does not create extra damage through negative defense. It may still help against a different opponent with higher defense.

My recommendation: if a damage dealer survives but struggles against a durable target, inspect the matching penetration before buying more attack. If that target's effective defense is already zero, more penetration will not improve this part of the hit.

Try it: where did my damage go?

Use Armor with Armor Penetration for a physical hit, or Magic Defense with Magic Penetration for a magical hit. “Damage before defense” is the result of the attack or skill formula, not necessarily your hero's Attack stat.

8,000 − 6,000 = 2,000 effective defense. It reduces damage by 40%. The 10,000 hit becomes 6,000 damage.

This isolates defense and penetration. It is not a full battle simulator and does not include other skills, critical hits, or damage modifiers.

Chance stats: a rating is not a percentage

This is one of the most useful distinctions in the game. 10,000 Critical Hit Chance does not mean 10,000%, 100%, or even the same chance against every enemy. The calculation compares the relevant stat with the opponent's main stat.

For these chance calculations: your relevant stat ÷ (your relevant stat + the opponent's main stat) × 100. If both numbers are 10,000, the result is 50%. If your rating stays at 10,000 but the opponent's main stat is 20,000, it becomes about 33.3%.

Use the value of the opponent's actual main stat, not their Power or the sum of Strength, Intelligence, and Agility. For a Strength-main opponent, that means their Strength value.

Dodge: a chance to avoid physical damage

Dodge is compared with the attacker's main stat. With 5,000 Dodge against an attacker whose main stat is 15,000, the chance is 25%. The hero can avoid a physical hit completely when Dodge succeeds, but this is not a guaranteed 25% reduction on every hit.

My concern with relying only on Dodge is consistency. The hit that gets through can still hurt. Health and appropriate defense give a fragile hero a fallback when luck does not cooperate. Dodge also does not provide a general answer to magical or pure damage.

Critical Hit Chance: double physical damage

A physical critical hit deals 2× damage. A hit worth 10,000 becomes 20,000 from the critical multiplier, keeping other conditions equal. Your Critical Hit Chance is compared with the target's main stat.

A 50% chance does not mean every second hit will crit. Several ordinary hits or several critical hits can occur in a row. I value critical chance when the hero already has enough underlying damage and penetration to make those successful criticals count.

Magical Critical Hit Chance: a different stat, a bigger multiplier

Magical critical hits deal 2.5× magical damage. A comparable 10,000-damage magical hit becomes 25,000 from that multiplier. The chance uses Magical Critical Hit Chance and the target's main stat.

Ordinary Critical Hit Chance does not substitute for this stat. Likewise, the magical version does not make physical or pure damage critical. Check which damage the hero actually deals before chasing an attractive critical bonus.

Turn a rating into a chance

For critical hits, enter the attacker's critical rating and the defender's main stat. For Dodge or Magic Reflection, enter the defender's rating and the attacker's main stat.

10,000 ÷ (10,000 + 10,000) × 100 = 50%. This is a chance per eligible event, not a guaranteed sequence.

Vampirism: damage that helps you stay alive

Vampirism restores Health equal to a percentage of the damage dealt by a basic attack or ability. If the hero deals 10,000 damage with 10% Vampirism, the healing is 1,000 Health.

The key words are damage dealt. If defenses reduce that damage to 4,000, 10% Vampirism produces 400 healing. Better damage can therefore improve sustain as well as offense.

I see Vampirism as support for a hero who keeps attacking. It cannot be your whole survival plan if the hero dies before dealing damage, spends the fight controlled, or cannot hurt the target enough. First make sure the hero gets the opportunity to use it.

The low-Health battle: finishing enemies and surviving pressure

Some fights change dramatically near the end of a Health bar. Crushing and Toughness work in that part of the battle. Their formulas resemble the chance formula, but their results describe damage changes, not the probability of a critical hit or dodge.

Crushing: help finish an enemy below 50% Health

Crushing increases outgoing damage to enemies whose Health is below 50%. It can affect physical, magical, and pure damage, excluding damage from allies' abilities.

The increase is Crushing ÷ (Crushing + target's main stat) × 100. With 5,000 Crushing against 15,000 main stat, the bonus is 25% while the condition applies. Isolating this bonus, a 10,000-damage hit becomes 12,500.

My reason to consider Crushing is straightforward: the team gets enemies low but struggles to finish them. It does not supply that same bonus throughout the enemy's full Health bar, so I would not treat it as an unconditional replacement for attack or penetration.

Toughness: reduce incoming damage below 50% Health

Toughness reduces incoming physical, magical, and pure damage when the hero's own Health is below 50%. Self-inflicted damage is excluded. Summoned entities, such as Morrigan's skeletons, have zero Toughness by default.

The reduction is Toughness ÷ (Toughness + attacker's main stat) × 100. With 5,000 Toughness against 15,000 main stat, the reduction is 25%. In an example isolating Toughness while it is active, 10,000 incoming damage becomes 7,500.

Keep the two Health checks separate: Crushing checks the enemy's Health; Toughness checks your hero's Health. I consider Toughness when a hero needs help staying alive in that vulnerable stage. It is not permanent protection at full Health.

Magic Reflection: sometimes the incoming spell comes back

Magic Reflection gives a chance to avoid an incoming magical hit completely and return part of its damage to the attacker. The reflection amount in this guide's stat reference is 20%; that is separate from the chance of activating the effect.

For the activation chance, compare the defender's Magic Reflection with the attacker's main stat. A rating of 5,000 against 15,000 main stat gives a 25% chance. On a successful reflection of an otherwise 10,000-damage hit, the hero avoids that hit and returns 2,000 damage under the 20% rule.

Reflected magical damage cannot be reflected again. Magic Reflection can activate while the hero is stunned, but it does not activate while the hero is silenced. It does not reflect physical or pure damage.

My view: this can be valuable against magical pressure, but I would not confuse its occasional full avoidance with the consistent reduction from Magic Defense. Consider the enemy's damage type and Silence effects before judging the upgrade.

How I would decide what to upgrade next

I would not give a tank, a healer, and a damage dealer the same priority list. Even two damage dealers can want different stats because their skill formulas, targets, and teammates differ.

Then I compare the cost. A useful stat is not automatically the best next purchase if another affordable upgrade fixes the same problem. I also account for the buffs the team actually provides, rather than treating each hero as if they fight alone.

After upgrading, compare similar battles. A single lucky critical hit or dodge can change one result. Look for a repeatable improvement: surviving to the skill, getting through the target, or finishing the fight more reliably.

The approach I recommend: give the hero enough survival to do their job, develop the stats their skills actually use, and adapt to the opponents you face. That gives every resource a purpose.

The questions I want you to leave with answered

Do I need to upgrade every stat equally?

No. Start with the hero's role, skill formulas, and the problem in your battles. Equal investment does not guarantee an effective build. Some stats contribute much more to a particular task than others.

Is 50% Critical Hit Chance the same as 50% more damage on every hit?

No. It is a chance to deal a critical hit. A physical critical doubles damage when it happens. An average over many eligible hits is different from what happens on the next attack.

Does penetration increase damage against every target?

It helps when the target still has the corresponding effective defense. Once that defense reaches zero, additional penetration adds no damage through this calculation against that target.

Can Armor protect against all types of damage?

No. Armor reduces physical damage and Magic Defense reduces magical damage. Pure damage does not use either defense calculation. Health and applicable effects such as Toughness have different roles.

Are damage over time and Stun also stats?

No. Damage over time describes a damage effect; Stun is a control effect. They affect how a fight unfolds, but they are not additional attributes to add to the hero's stat list. A skill's own rules still matter when you apply general stat explanations.

Where can I check my hero's actual stats?

Open Heroes, select the hero, and tap their name or Power in the Hero tab to open their stats. Use the values there when comparing builds, then consider the temporary effects present in the battle.

My final advice: make the next upgrade answer a question

You do not need to become a mathematician to build a better team. Start with one question: “Why did this hero fail to do their job?” The answer might be insufficient penetration, the wrong defense, a skill that scales with another stat, or an enemy who prevents the hero from acting.

That is the point where the stat screen becomes useful. Instead of a wall of numbers, it becomes a set of choices you can explain. Before spending your next batch of resources, pick the problem you want to solve. Then watch whether that part of the fight improves.
