Why the Realm’s Brown Squares Matter So Much

The center is the most valuable part of the map because its resource locations are higher level. Wood, food, stone, and iron arrive faster, helping your guild upgrade sooner. Stronger monsters and nearby rally bosses also make this area more efficient for active players. The important detail is not one spectacular reward; it is the steady stream of small advantages that continues while your guild holds a good position.

I believe marching time is one of the most underestimated resources in Realm. Every minute your heroes spend crossing the map is a minute they are not gathering or fighting. Shorter routes let members complete more actions with the same playtime, and across an entire guild and several days, those saved minutes become hours of extra progress.

The Snowball Effect Starts Earlier Than You Think

Better gathering creates faster upgrades; faster upgrades make the guild stronger; and that strength makes it easier to defeat valuable targets and defend important territory. This is the Realm snowball effect. A small lead during the opening days can become a gap that other guilds struggle to close later in the season.

That is why I would not judge a position only by how comfortable it looks right now. Ask what it will produce after three, five, or ten days. A quiet starting area may protect you from early conflict, but it can also make every upgrade slower while ambitious guilds compound their advantage in the center.

Should Your Guild Teleport to the Center?

For a guild that wants to compete seriously, my answer is yes—move early and move together. A coordinated relocation gives members access to the same valuable zone and helps the guild claim useful positions before the map becomes crowded. If only a few players teleport while everyone else stays behind, the guild loses much of the strategic benefit.

Timing matters because the four brown squares will not remain peaceful. Other guilds can see the same opportunity, and the strongest groups will eventually fight over it. Arriving early does not guarantee victory, but arriving late means facing established rivals while trying to find whatever space remains.

The Risk Smaller and Casual Guilds Must Consider

I do not think every guild should rush into the exact center without a plan. The rewards are excellent, but competition will be much tougher. A smaller or more casual guild should compare its activity, coordination, and ability to defend itself against the likely opposition. Sometimes the smartest position is close enough to benefit from shorter marches without standing directly in the path of the largest guilds.

Talk with your guild before anyone spends a teleport. Choose a target area, agree on a time, and make sure members understand the reason for moving. Realm rewards teamwork more than isolated decisions, and a clear strategy can turn an ordinary guild into a difficult neighbor to displace.

My Verdict: Do Not Let a Safe Start Become a Slow Defeat

In my opinion, location matters far more than many players realize. If your guild wants to challenge the leaders, moving toward the brown squares during the first few days could be one of the most important decisions of the season. You still need activity, coordination, and strength—but the center gives all three more room to grow.
