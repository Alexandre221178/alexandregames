You do not need to spend big to build a strong team in Hero Wars Alliance, but you do need to choose carefully. Upgrading every new hero can make your account's total power look impressive while leaving your main team too weak to progress.

The four lineups below are the paths I recommend considering as a free-to-play player or low spender. Each has a different farming route and its own resource bottlenecks. My first choice among them is Leonel, Byrna, Somna, Folio and Polaris, but the right choice for your account also depends on the heroes you already own and the rewards you can access.

Choose one path first. Build a reliable main team, then develop a second and a third before expanding into heroes for specific matchups. These are four alternatives to compare, not four projects to start together.

A strong meta team still needs a realistic build path

One of my strong meta picks is Dorian, Cleaver, Byrna, Aidan and Kendle. That does not automatically make it my first recommendation for a new F2P account. A lineup is useful only if you can actually obtain its heroes and keep developing them.

Cleaver's guaranteed unlock comes at 400 Heroic Chest openings, which can take months depending on your rewards. Kendle's Soul Stone events have already passed, and neither Kendle nor Aidan has a regular shop source in the farming options discussed here. Dorian can be purchased with gold, and Byrna is available through Realm, but those two accessible heroes do not remove the other acquisition hurdles.

That is why I separate a team I like in the meta from a team I would advise a developing account to build. The following options give you a clearer route for your resources, with the conditions explained for each one.

1. Leonel, Byrna, Somna, Folio and Polaris

This is my main recommendation among these four paths. Leonel, Byrna, Somna and Polaris have Soul Stones available through Realm, while Folio comes from the Arena Shop. Rather than chasing several unrelated heroes, you can work toward a complete lineup through a small number of activities.

Realm unlocks at team level 40, so this is a team to build toward as your account progresses. Realm is still being developed, and its rewards and progression details may change. The Alexandre Games Realm guide confirms the current unlock level and explains its progression systems.

Leonel is particularly interesting because Realm provides several materials for his development, including Soul Stones, artifact materials and Skin Stones. His Talisman also comes through Realm progression, although reaching it takes time. Having a route for more than just Soul Stones makes him easier to plan around.

Somna and Folio have another advantage: their relic materials are among the rewards available through Guild Versus relic coffers earned from daily missions. Keep collecting those rewards while you develop the team. The official Guild Versus guide explains that Relic Caskets award random hero shards, so this is gradual progress rather than a guaranteed daily amount for one hero.

Byrna and Polaris are two heroes I rate highly and like building around. Polaris is already useful without a relic, which gives you room to develop the lineup while working toward later upgrades.

Later variations with Guus or Aidan

If you obtain Guus or Aidan, you can explore Leonel, Byrna, Guus, Folio and Polaris, or Leonel, Byrna, Guus, Polaris and Aidan. Treat those as future options. Your starting plan should match what you can farm now, rather than depend on an unavailable replacement.

2. Electra, Dante, Iris, Dorian and Octavia

This is another lineup I like for a developing account. Electra comes from Realm, Dante from the Tower Shop, and Dorian has Soul Stones available for gold. Iris and Octavia are both in the Grand Arena Shop, so they compete for the same currency. You can check their availability in the official Grand Arena Shop list.

The shared shop is the main planning issue here. Decide which hero needs the next star upgrade most, rather than expecting to maximize both immediately. Dorian also puts pressure on your gold, which you still need for other upgrades across the team.

Electra and Dante have relic materials available in the free Guild Versus coffers. For Iris, Dorian and Octavia, you may need events to collect the relevant materials. I still like this composition without their relics; obtaining the heroes and completing every advanced upgrade are separate stages of the build.

If you obtain Crow, he is another option I like in Octavia's place: Electra, Dante, Iris, Dorian and Crow. This version adds pressure while Electra absorbs damage and Iris benefits from Dorian's vampirism. It is an alternative to test once Crow is available to your account.

3. Julius, Judge, Nebula, Isaac and Eva

I like this Progress core, but Eva makes the recommendation conditional. Her Soul Stones have appeared in recent events, so check whether you can obtain and develop her before committing to the full lineup.

The other four heroes have shop sources: Julius and Nebula come from the Guild War Shop, Judge from the Arena Shop, and Isaac from the Dungeon Shop. This gives much of the team a clear farming route, although Julius and Nebula share a currency and will need a purchase priority.

Do not confuse access to Soul Stones with equal access to every upgrade. Skins, artifacts and relics can create different bottlenecks even when a hero is easy to buy from a shop. Judge the project by the resources your whole lineup needs, including Eva's availability.

Polaris can also be tested in Isaac's position, depending on the matchup. That is a variation to explore as your roster grows, rather than a reason to split your early investment between both versions.

4. Satori, Byrna, Lian, Amira and Soleil

This is an alternative for players who enjoy Satori teams or already have some of these heroes developed. I like Satori as an explosive magic damage dealer, and an existing investment in this group can make the path more practical than starting a completely different team.

Satori comes from the Dungeon Shop, Byrna from Realm, and Lian from the Grand Arena Shop. Amira and Soleil come from Eternal Frontier.

Amira and Soleil share a currency, so choose which one to prioritize. As with Iris and Octavia, a clear source does not mean you can finish both heroes immediately. Plan their purchases around the team's next useful upgrade.

I would consider this path especially carefully if you already have a developed Satori or several of his teammates. Your current roster matters: a team you can strengthen consistently is often a better project than an attractive lineup you cannot yet assemble.

How to prioritize your upgrades

The biggest mistake is spreading resources across too many heroes and titans at once. I also play Hero Wars: Dominion Era in a F2P guild, and I see the same habit there. For this Alliance guide, the principle is simple: make your first team reliable before you start funding several more.

Within that team, investment does not need to be equal. Look at what is preventing progress. If a key hero cannot survive long enough to contribute, address that weakness. If your damage is falling behind, focus on the upgrades that help your damage dealer do its job. Spend toward a useful result rather than raising every hero's power evenly.

Keep Soul Stone farming, shared shop currencies and relic access in the same plan. Save a purchase priority for pairs such as Iris and Octavia, Julius and Nebula, or Amira and Soleil. Collect your daily rewards consistently, and treat event-dependent heroes as conditional choices until you have access to them.

Once your main team is established, build your second and then your third. After that, add specialists and test substitutions for particular opponents. Farmable heroes still take time to develop, but a focused plan helps every resource contribute to your progress.

For a new project, my first pick from these options remains Leonel, Byrna, Somna, Folio and Polaris. If your account already supports another path, use that advantage. Choose one team, strengthen it, and expand gradually.
